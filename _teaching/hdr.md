A PhD is your opportunity to make an original contribution to physics, and to become the world's leading expert on something wonderfully specific. You work closely with a supervisor on a substantial research project, develop expertise in your field, and write a thesis based on your own research. Our PhD candidates work across all of the discipline's [research areas]({{ '/#research' | relative_url }}).

## What the PhD involves

- **Duration:** 3.5 years of full-time equivalent enrolment. Part-time enrolment may also be possible.
- **Research:** you develop and carry out your own research project with the guidance of your supervisors, and write it up as a thesis.
- **No teaching requirement:** there is no expectation that PhD candidates teach. Many choose to gain experience as lab demonstrators or tutors, which is paid in addition to any scholarship.
- **Research masters:** a Master of Philosophy is possible in some cases, though we rarely take on masters candidates. Please discuss this with a potential supervisor.

## Completing your PhD

Along the way you'll need to meet the University's requirements for PhD candidates:

- **Induction and training:** short compulsory induction modules in your first weeks, including research integrity and health and safety.
- **Confirmation of candidature:** usually within your first year, you submit a written research proposal, give a presentation and discuss your plans with a review panel.
- **Progress reviews:** a progress report within your first six months, then annual reports for the rest of your candidature.
- **Thesis:** you submit your thesis within 3.5 years (full-time equivalent). A thesis by publication, built around your published papers, is also possible.
- **Examination:** your thesis is assessed by independent expert examiners. The PhD is awarded once they recommend it and any corrections are complete.

## Contact a supervisor before you apply

**You must contact a potential supervisor before applying.** Applications cannot proceed without a supervisor who has agreed to support your project.

PhD projects are much broader and more innovative than undergraduate projects, and they are shaped together with the candidate. For that reason we don't list specific PhD projects here. Every academic in the discipline is open to supervising PhD students: browse [our people]({{ '/#people' | relative_url }}) and [research areas]({{ '/#research' | relative_url }}), find someone whose work interests you, and email them to start the conversation. It helps to include a short CV, your academic transcript, and a few sentences on the kind of research you'd like to do.

## Scholarships and funding

- **Domestic students** can apply for a Research Training Program (RTP) scholarship. This covers tuition fees and provides a living stipend, currently **$40,690 per year (2027 rate)** for up to 3.5 years.
- **International students** can apply directly to a supervisor who has research funding available to support a PhD candidate. Some international scholarship schemes are also available through the University.

Your potential supervisor can advise you on the funding options that suit your situation.
