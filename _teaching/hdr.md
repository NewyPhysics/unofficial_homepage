A PhD is your opportunity to make an original contribution to physics. You work closely with a supervisor on a substantial research project, develop expertise in your field, and write a thesis based on your own research. Our PhD candidates work across all of the discipline's [research areas]({{ '/#research' | relative_url }}).

## What the PhD involves

- **Duration:** 3.5 years of full-time equivalent enrolment. Part-time enrolment may also be possible.
- **Research:** you develop and carry out your own research project with the guidance of your supervisors, and write it up as a thesis.
- **Research masters:** a Master of Philosophy is possible in some cases, though we rarely take on masters candidates. Please discuss this with a potential supervisor.

## Contact a supervisor before you apply

**You must contact a potential supervisor before applying.** Applications cannot proceed without a supervisor who has agreed to support your project.

PhD projects are much broader and more innovative than undergraduate projects, and they are shaped together with the candidate. For that reason we don't list specific PhD projects here. Every academic in the discipline is open to supervising PhD students: browse [our people]({{ '/#people' | relative_url }}) and [research areas]({{ '/#research' | relative_url }}), find someone whose work interests you, and email them to start the conversation. It helps to include a short CV, your academic transcript, and a few sentences on the kind of research you'd like to do.

## Scholarships and funding

- **Domestic students** can apply for a Research Training Program (RTP) scholarship, which covers tuition fees and can provide a living stipend.
- **International students** can apply directly to a supervisor who has research funding available to support a PhD candidate. Some international scholarship schemes are also available through the University.

Your potential supervisor can advise you on the funding options that suit your situation.
