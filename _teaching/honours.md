Honours is an extra year of advanced study taken after you finish your Bachelor of Science (known as "end-on" Honours). It is your chance to try out real research: you work on your own project with an academic supervisor, write a thesis, and take advanced physics coursework alongside it.

Honours is also the usual pathway into a research degree. It shows you, and future supervisors, whether research suits you before you commit to a PhD, and a strong Honours result is the standard route to PhD entry and scholarships. Many Honours graduates go straight into a PhD; others take their research, analysis and communication skills into industry, government or teaching.

## What the year involves

- **Duration:** one year of full-time study, based at the Callaghan campus.
- **Size:** 80 units of advanced (4000-level) study.
- **Structure:** roughly half research and half coursework. The research project runs across the year and finishes with a written thesis and presentation, while the coursework builds advanced physics knowledge.
- **Physics Honours courses:** currently Physics Honours 421 (PHYS4210) and Physics Honours 422 (PHYS4220). Check the Handbook for current codes.
- **Result:** Honours is awarded with a class (Class I, Class II Division 1, Class II Division 2 or Class III) based on your coursework and research results.

## Entry requirements

- **A completed degree:** a Bachelor of Science with a major in physics, or a related degree.
- **GPA:** a minimum GPA of 5.0 on the University's 7-point scale. Depending on the program rules, this can be calculated across your whole degree or across your later-year courses.
- **Physics background:** the assumed knowledge for Physics Honours is a physics major with at least a Credit average in 3000-level physics courses.
- **A supervisor and project:** you need an academic supervisor who agrees to take you on, and a project that fits their research.

The exact rules are set out in the University Handbook for each year, so please check there before applying.

## Choosing a project

Start by browsing our [undergraduate research projects]({{ '/projects/undergraduate-research/' | relative_url }}). Projects are flexible and can be adapted to your interests. Then talk to potential supervisors early, ideally during your third year. You're welcome to email them or drop by the Physics building on the Callaghan campus (our offices are upstairs).

## How to apply

Applications are made online through the University. For a Semester 1, 2027 start, applications close at 11:59pm on Friday 5 February 2027, and late applications are not accepted.
